## 🌱 The Mission

Welcome to **POTD-LeetCode** — a daily commitment to becoming a better problem solver, one challenge at a time.

This repository is a record of consistency, curiosity, and growth. Every problem solved is more than a submission: it is practice in breaking down complexity, recognizing patterns, learning from mistakes, and thinking with clarity.

The goal isn't to solve everything overnight. The goal is to show up, learn something new, and improve a little every single day.

## 🎯 What You'll Find Here

- **Daily challenges:** A dedicated place to organize the Problem of the Day.
- **Problem-solving practice:** A growing collection of approaches and solutions.
- **Learning through iteration:** Better logic, cleaner code, and more efficient solutions over time.
- **Consistency over perfection:** Progress matters more than an occasional perfect streak.

## 📂 Repository Structure

```text
POTD-LeetCode/
├── Problem of the Day/
│   └── README.md
└── README.md
```

Add each day's solution and notes inside **Problem of the Day**, organized by date or problem name as the collection grows.

## 🧠 The Daily Commitment

1. **Understand** the problem before writing code.
2. **Think** of a straightforward approach, then look for patterns and optimizations.
3. **Solve** it independently before checking hints or editorials.
4. **Analyze** time and space complexity.
5. **Reflect** on what the problem taught you.
6. **Repeat** tomorrow. Keep the momentum alive.

## 🔥 A Reminder for the Hard Days

Some problems will feel impossible. Some solutions will take hours. Some days, the only progress will be understanding why an approach doesn't work.

That still counts.

Every wrong answer is feedback. Every bug is a lesson. Every difficult problem is an opportunity to build patience and resilience. Don't compare your Day 1 to someone else's Day 1000.

**You do not need to be the fastest. You just need to keep moving.**

> “It always seems impossible until it's done.” — Nelson Mandela

## 📈 Measure Progress by Growth

The real achievement is not just the number of problems solved. It is the ability to:
- Recognize familiar patterns in unfamiliar problems.
- Explain why a solution works.
- Choose the right data structure and algorithm.
- Write readable, maintainable code.
- Stay consistent even when motivation fades.

## ⭐ The Rule

**Never let one difficult problem become a reason to quit. Let it become the reason you get better.**

One problem a day. One concept at a time. One step closer to your goals.
